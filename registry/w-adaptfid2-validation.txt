   176	location and against inputs the harness cannot modify**:
   177	
   178	1. **The verifier's definition lives in the dataset**, never in the task dict and
   179	   never written into the working directory.
   180	2. **It runs after the harness has exited**, in its own sandbox invocation, with
   181	   the harness's artifacts mounted **read-only**.
   182	3. **Its inputs are re-materialized at verification time.** If the task ships tests,
   183	   they are written again before the check runs, overwriting whatever the harness
   184	   left. Without this, editing the tests is the cheapest way to score.
   185	4. **The result is one score per task, produced by the platform.**
   186	
   187	**Exit code and score are independent.** The harness's exit code says whether the
   188	*harness* finished; the verifier says whether the *task* was completed. A harness
   189	that crashed but left working artifacts still scores — the platform grades the
   190	result, not the process. (This was already true: `eval/run/runner.py` recorded
   191	`exit_code` and never used it in the score.)
   192	
   193	**The harness may run the task's tests itself**, for its own feedback. That is its
   194	own copy and the platform does not trust it; the authoritative check uses the
   195	platform's re-materialized inputs.
   196	
   197	**That is the *write* half, and the read half is a different rule** — worth stating
   198	because the two are easy to conflate. Something placed in `SETUP` is **readable by the
   199	harness**; that is what `SETUP` is for, and it is why a task can ship its own tests.
   200	`VERIFY`'s `inputs` are not readable: they are written *after* the harness has exited,
   201	so a harness that looks for them during its run finds whatever was in `SETUP`, or
   202	nothing. A dataset that wants a check the harness cannot even *read* — not merely one
   203	it cannot *edit* — must therefore put it **only** in `verify.inputs`. Both halves are
   204	deliberate and neither is the default: shipping a check in `SETUP` is what lets a
   205	harness see whether it is passing, and withholding it is what stops a harness from
   206	fitting itself to the answer. A dataset that puts the same check in both has chosen to
   207	show it, whatever it intended.
